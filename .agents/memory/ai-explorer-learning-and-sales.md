---
name: Learn Think Build learning and sales scope
description: Product goals for children's AI literacy and parent-led learning at home.
---

Learn Think Build is a parent-focused, at-home introduction to AI for learners starting at age 10, with recommended paths for ages 10–12, 13–15, and 16+. The goal is to help children safely build AI skills they will need in the real world, gradually and through age-appropriate lessons. Position it as a creative alternative to passive screen time: encourage children to make and build with AI safely, not only consume content. Use game-inspired progress, mastery scores, personal bests, and badges to reward learning and creation without pressure streaks or rewarding time spent. Do not advertise scores, badges, or game features until implemented. The year-one learning goal is for a learner to build and explain a basic app. Advancement should be based on demonstrated understanding and passing assessments, not lesson completion alone. Teach prompt writing, checking AI outputs, appropriate use, privacy, and academic integrity—including when AI use is cheating. Each lesson should integrate narration with visuals, interaction, retrieval, reflection, and spaced review; do not match learners to a supposed visual or auditory learning style. Do not add an open-ended live AI chatbot for learners; simulate AI examples in lessons. Real AI tools should only be introduced with strict parent or adult supervision. Planned family pricing is $9.99 per month. Do not position the product for school sales or add school licensing workflows. Do not connect Stripe or enable billing until the LLC details are ready; the goal in the meantime is to get the app URL live, with checkout clearly marked as coming soon. Public testimonials must be genuine family statements approved for publication; never invent reviews.

**Why:** The user wants Learn Think Build marketed to parents for at-home AI introduction, with gradual practical skills, age-appropriate detail, and a playful shift from passive screen time toward safe creation.

**Illustrative quote exception:** The user requested made-up quotes about how helpful, fun, and intuitive the app is, rather than an empty testimonials page, and clarified that the quotes should be from parents describing useful lessons. These are illustrative marketing examples, not genuine testimonials.

**Why:** Preserve the requested positive copy without misrepresenting fictional statements as real customer endorsements.

**How to apply:** Clearly label fictional quotes as illustrative and not real reviews. Do not add fabricated names, ratings, or claims of verified users. The genuine-testimonial rules still apply to real endorsements.

**How to apply:** Market the product to parents and guardians for guided learning at home. Make lessons progressively richer by age and mastery; use game mechanics to reward understanding and building, not pressure or screen time. Describe high scores, badges, and personal-best features as planned until live. Use integrated learning modes in every lesson rather than learning-style matching. Keep classroom/teacher rules only where they support responsible-use lessons, not as a school-sales pitch. Use authentic, permissioned family testimonials only. Preserve the distinction between planned curriculum/tool access and features currently implemented. Keep internal artifact and billing identifiers stable unless they are intentionally migrated. Keep the learning app independent from Stripe until the LLC details are ready.
